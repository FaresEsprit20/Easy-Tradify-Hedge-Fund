How to read the numbers

Frame. Directional readings are converted to the trade's side: “RSI says SELL” is ALIGNED on a short and OPPOSED on a long. RSI, stochastic %K and Bollinger %B are mirrored for shorts, so “< 30” always means the trade side was oversold.

Edge is expectancy when ALIGNED minus expectancy when OPPOSED, in R. A negative edge means the component was inverted on this sample. Edges built on fewer than 10 trades on either side are marked small.

p and q. p comes from 2,000 outcome shuffles that repeat the same “pick the best state” search, so choosing the flattering bucket is paid for. q applies Benjamini–Hochberg across every component and category on the page.

SELL share. Shorts outperformed longs in this window. A state made mostly of one direction can look predictive just by restating the direction; the share is shown on each cell.

Costs. Price R ignores commission. Net R divides the booked result by the broker's loss at the stop; the gap (≈0.58R per trade) is the round-trip commission on stops of 1–2 pips.
